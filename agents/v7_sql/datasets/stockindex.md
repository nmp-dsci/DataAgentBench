- `stockindex_index_trade` has 13 distinct index symbols, with no nulls or non-positive values in "Close". No table stores the country or region: map each symbol to its exchange and country from geographic knowledge. `stockindex_index_info` lists 14 exchanges by full name with a 3-letter currency code. Its 'Korea Exchange' (KS11) has no rows in the trade table. Run `select distinct "Index"` to see the symbols before writing any mapping, and cover every one of them.
- "Date" is text in three shapes, and all rows parse: 'YYYY-MM-DD HH24:MI:SS' (use the first 10 chars), 'DD Mon YYYY, HH24:MI' (the first 11 chars), and 'Month DD, YYYY at HH12:MI AM' (the part before ' at ', format FMMonth DD, YYYY). Pick each shape with a regex and parse to a real date before any filter by date, min/max or first/last-row logic. Series start in different years (1960s to 2000s), so "since YEAR" means rows with parsed date >= that year's Jan 1, taken per index.
- "Close" and "Adj Close" differ on about 13k rows. "Close" is in local currency and "CloseUSD" is in USD. Use "Close" unless the question says adjusted or USD.
- "Overall return" / "growth" of an index over a period is read in this dataset as buy-and-hold: the Close on the index's latest date in the window divided by the Close on its earliest date in the window. Use that simple last/first ratio even when the question wraps it in an investing story (e.g. regular or monthly investing). When the question asks only which indices and their country, return just the symbol and country columns.
