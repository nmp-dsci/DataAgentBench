- Title output: gold answers CSV-encode every returned title column, whatever the question's wording. If a title contains a comma or a double quote, wrap it in double quotes and double any inner quotes. Leave other titles plain. About 34 of the 200 titles contain a comma. Apply this in pass_through mode too.
- Join key: `books_info.book_id` is `bookid_N` and `review.purchase_id` is `purchaseid_N`. Extract the trailing integer on both sides (`substring(x from '[0-9]+$')::int`) and compare the integers.
- `categories` is a string-encoded list, e.g. `["Books", "Literature & Fiction", "Poetry"]`. Match a category on its exact quoted element (regex on `"Name"`), not with a loose LIKE.
- Book language is found only in the templated `details` prose, which has many phrasings ("written in English", "available in both English and X", "this English-language ..."). Match with a word-bounded regex (`\mEnglish\M`). Bilingual editions still count.
- Dates about a book (publication/release) have no column. They appear in the `details` prose as month-anchored dates ("Month YYYY" or "Month D, YYYY"); most books have one. Some `subtitle` values also carry a date ("Paperback - Month D, YYYY"). Bare 4-digit numbers in `details` are not years: page counts and templated "NNNNth edition" text produce them too.
- `review_time` is text in one consistent format; cast it with `::timestamp`.
- Average rating per book = `avg(rating)` over individual `bookreview_review` rows joined by the extracted id. Never use `rating_number` or any other pre-aggregated field on `books_info`. Every book has at least 1 review.
